Timeline guides

Know exactly
what comes next.

Step-by-step playbooks for the three moves people make in real estate.
Pick your path.

Buying in the East Bay moves fast and competitively. Here’s the path from “thinking about it” to holding the keys.

StartPre-approval
SearchTour & offer
Under contractEscrow & due diligence
FinishSign, record & keys
Usually involved: You · Agent · Lender · Escrow · Inspectors
Phase 1
Prepare
Get financially ready and define the search before touring.
1
Before touring

Get pre-approved

Know your real budget before you start shopping.

+

Talk to a lender before you shop. A pre-approval tells you your real budget and makes your offers competitive. Gather pay stubs, W-2s, and bank statements.

Your focus: Income documents, cash available, monthly-payment comfort.
People: You · Lender · Agent
2
Before touring

Define what you actually want

Turn a broad search into a clear shortlist.

+

City, size, commute, schools, must-haves vs. nice-to-haves. Not sure where to start? The City Match Quiz and Price Map narrow it down fast.

Your focus: Must-haves, deal-breakers, commute, budget, neighborhood feel.
People: You · Agent
Phase 2
Shop & compete
Tour, refine quickly, and build an offer around the specific property.
3
Search phase

Tour homes & refine

Real homes make your priorities much clearer.

+

See a range in person. Your priorities sharpen quickly once you’re walking real homes, and we adjust the search as they do.

Your focus: Compare condition, location, layout, monthly cost, and trade-offs.
People: You · Agent
4
Offer day

Make a strong offer

Price matters, but the full package determines strength.

+

Price, contingencies, close timeline, and terms all matter, not just the number. In a competitive market, strategy wins the home.

Your focus: Decide your ceiling and which terms you are comfortable with.
People: You · Agent · Lender
East Bay note: Competition can vary dramatically by city, neighborhood, condition, and price point. The strategy should fit the specific listing rather than using one template for every offer.
Phase 3
Under contract
Escrow opens and the financial, physical, and contractual review happens.
5
After acceptance

Open escrow & deposit

The accepted offer becomes an active transaction.

+

Once your offer’s accepted, you’ll wire an earnest money deposit and a neutral escrow holder opens the file to manage funds and paperwork.

Your focus: Watch deadlines, verify wiring instructions, and stay responsive.
People: You · Agent · Escrow · Lender
6
Early escrow

Inspections, disclosures & appraisal

Verify the property, the documents, and the lender’s value.

+

Do your due diligence, home inspection, disclosures, and the lender’s appraisal. If issues surface, we review the options and negotiate where appropriate.

Your focus: Understand defects, costs, disclosures, HOA documents when applicable, and the appraisal.
People: You · Agent · Inspectors · Lender / Appraiser
East Bay note: Insurance availability, HOA or special-assessment documents, local property conditions, and new-construction contracts can materially change the due-diligence picture. Review the documents for the actual property rather than relying on assumptions.
7
During escrow

Loan underwriting & final approval

Keep your finances stable while the lender finishes the file.

+

Your lender verifies everything and issues final loan approval. Avoid big purchases or new credit during this window.

Your focus: Send requested documents quickly and avoid financial changes.
People: You · Lender · Agent · Escrow
Phase 4
Close
Confirm the property, sign the final documents, fund, and record.
8
Closing week

Final walkthrough

One last confirmation before the transaction finishes.

+

A last look to confirm the home’s condition and that any agreed repairs are done before you sign.

Your focus: Confirm condition, included items, and agreed work.
People: You · Agent
9
Closing day

Sign, fund & get the keys

The loan funds, the deed records, and ownership transfers.

+

You sign, the loan funds, the deed records, and the home is yours. Welcome to the East Bay.

Your focus: Final signatures, funds, utilities, move-in plan.
People: You · Agent · Lender · Escrow

Phase 1
Plan the sale
Set the goal, timeline, positioning, and pricing strategy before work begins.
1
Before listing

Strategy call & goals

Build the sale around what needs to happen next in your life.

+

We start with your timeline, your reasons for selling, and what a win looks like, then build the plan backward from there.

Your focus: Move date, next-home plan, priorities, condition, and risk tolerance.
People: You · Agent
2
Pricing phase

Price it with real data

Position the home against the market buyers are actually seeing.

+

A comparative market analysis of recent nearby sales sets a price that attracts buyers without leaving money on the table.

Your focus: Understand comparable sales, active competition, condition, and pricing trade-offs.
People: You · Agent
Phase 2
Prepare & launch
Make the property show its best, then launch with consistent presentation.
3
Pre-launch

Prep & stage

Focus effort where buyers will notice it most.

+

Repairs, cleaning, decluttering, and staging where it counts. Small, targeted improvements often return the most at sale.

Your focus: Safety / defect items, curb appeal, cleanliness, clutter, presentation.
People: You · Agent · Contractors / stager as needed
Seller note: Not every upgrade has the same payoff. Prioritize improvements that remove buyer objections or materially improve presentation instead of remodeling by default.
4
Launch week

Market the listing

Create one strong presentation everywhere buyers discover the home.

+

Professional photos, a compelling listing, MLS syndication, and targeted promotion put your home in front of the right buyers.

Your focus: Final condition, showing access, launch timing, and communication plan.

Phase 3
Market & negotiate
Turn buyer activity into the best combination of price, certainty, and terms.
5
On market

Showings & offers

Track real buyer response, not just clicks and views.

+

Open houses and private tours generate offers. We review each on price, terms, and strength, not just the top-line number.

Your focus: Keep the home show-ready and evaluate feedback objectively.
People: You · Agent · Buyers / buyer agents
6
Offer review

Negotiate & accept

Choose the offer with the strongest overall outcome, not automatically the highest number.

+

We counter and negotiate to protect your bottom line, then open escrow with the strongest buyer.

Your focus: Net proceeds, financing strength, contingencies, timing, and certainty.
People: You · Agent · Buyer side
Phase 4
Escrow & close
Manage buyer contingencies, final documents, recording, and the handoff.
7
In escrow

Buyer contingencies

Keep inspections, appraisal, financing, and requests moving toward removal.

+

The buyer completes inspections and appraisal. We manage requests and keep the deal on track to closing.

Your focus: Respond to requests, maintain the property, and prepare for the move.
People: You · Agent · Buyer side · Escrow
East Bay note: Disclosure packages, HOA documents when applicable, property condition, and insurance-related questions can influence negotiations during escrow. Preparing complete documents early reduces surprises.
8
Closing

Close & hand over keys

Final signatures, recording, proceeds, and possession complete the sale.

+

Final signatures, the deed records, proceeds are disbursed, and the sale is complete.

Your focus: Sign, move out as agreed, transfer utilities, and complete the handoff.
People: You · Agent · Escrow · Buyer side

Keeping your home and renting it out can build long-term wealth, but it comes with real responsibilities. Here’s how to approach it deliberately.

StartLease vs. sell
PrepareRent-ready & compliant
Place tenantMarket & screen
FinishMove-in & manage
Usually involved: You · Agent / property manager · Vendors · Applicants / tenant
Phase 1
Make the decision
Confirm that keeping the property as a rental fits the numbers and your goals.
1
Decision phase

Decide: lease or sell

Run the property like an investment before committing to landlord life.

+

Run the numbers on cash flow, appreciation, and tax before you commit. The Investment ROI calculator is a good first gut-check.

Your focus: Expected rent, mortgage, expenses, cash flow, reserves, and your next move.
People: You · Agent / property manager · Tax professional when needed
Phase 2
Prepare & price
Get the home ready, establish rent, and confirm the rules before marketing.
2
Before marketing

Get the property rent-ready

Address habitability, safety, maintenance, and presentation first.

+

Handle repairs, safety items (smoke/CO detectors, water heater strapping), deep cleaning, and anything that affects habitability.

Your focus: Repairs, safety items, cleanliness, appliances, access, and documentation.
People: You · Agent / property manager · Vendors
3
Pricing phase

Set the right rent

Price for the current rental market, not the number you hope to get.

+

Price against comparable rentals nearby. Too high sits empty; too low leaves money on the table. Market data sets the number.

Your focus: Comparable rentals, condition, amenities, vacancy risk, and target tenant.
People: You · Agent / property manager
4
Before listing

Know the legal rules

Build compliance into the process before an applicant ever applies.

+

California landlord-tenant law is strict; required disclosures, security-deposit limits, and statewide rent caps (AB 1482) may apply. Get these right up front.

Your focus: Confirm which rules, notices, disclosures, and exemptions apply to this property.
People: You · Agent / property manager · Appropriate legal professional when needed
California compliance note: Rental rules can change and can depend on the property, tenancy, and local jurisdiction. Treat this guide as a planning overview and verify the current requirements that apply to the actual rental.
Phase 3
Market & screen
Create demand, apply consistent criteria, and choose a qualified tenant.
5
Marketing

Market & show the rental

Good presentation and clear information improve applicant quality.

+

Good photos and a clear listing on the major rental sites bring qualified applicants quickly.

Your focus: Photos, accurate terms, showing access, application process, and response time.
People: You · Agent / property manager · Applicants
6
Application phase

Screen tenants carefully

Use consistent, lawful criteria and document the process.

+

Consistent, lawful screening — credit, income, rental history, and references — protects your property and your cash flow.

Your focus: Apply the same written criteria consistently and verify supporting information.
People: You · Agent / property manager · Applicants
Phase 4
Lease & manage
Document move-in carefully, then decide how the property will be operated day to day.
7
Before move-in

Lease & move-in

Put the agreement and property condition in writing before possession.

+

Use a solid written lease, collect the deposit, and document the home’s condition with a dated walkthrough and photos.

Your focus: Lease documents, funds, keys, condition record, utilities, and move-in communication.
People: You · Agent / property manager · Tenant
8
Ongoing

Manage it, or hand it off

Choose a repeatable system for rent, maintenance, communication, and compliance.

+

Stay on top of maintenance, rent collection, and compliance, or hire a property manager (typically ~8% of rent) to run it for you.

Your focus: Reserves, maintenance response, rent collection, records, renewals, and ongoing compliance.
People: You or property manager · Tenant · Vendors
